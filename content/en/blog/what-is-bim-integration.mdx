# What is BIM Integration and why is it different from BIM modeling?

In the construction industry, there is a frequent confusion: many companies claim to do "BIM" when they are actually doing three-dimensional modeling. The difference between both concepts is not technical — it is strategic. And it has direct consequences on project cost, schedule and quality.

## BIM Modeling: geometry without governance

BIM modeling produces digital geometry. A three-dimensional building. Walls, slabs, installations represented in a virtual environment. It is useful. It is necessary. But it is not sufficient.

The problem with modeling without integration is that it produces **scattered information**:

- Each discipline works in its independent model
- Models are not systematically coordinated
- Information has no governance structure
- Deliverables are not traceable or verifiable
- The model does not respond to the client's information requirements

The result: a set of files that represent the project, but do not **manage** the project.

## BIM Integration: coordinated and constructible information

BIM integration is a higher level of maturity. It involves:

**1. Information governance**
Defining who produces what information, when, in what format and with what level of detail. This is established in the `EIR` (Employer Information Requirements) and the `BEP` (BIM Execution Plan).

**2. Common Data Environment (CDE)**
A structured platform where all project information flows with controlled review, approval and distribution states. Not folders in Dropbox. A CDE under ISO 19650.

**3. Multidisciplinary coordination**
Architecture, structural, MEP and special systems models are integrated into a federated model. Interferences are detected, documented and resolved before construction through BCF workflows.

**4. Full traceability**
Every design decision, model change and deliverable has a date, an owner and a status. Information is traceable from inception to asset delivery.

## Why does this difference matter?

Projects that only have BIM modeling frequently face:

- **On-site interferences** that were not detected because models were not coordinated
- **Costly rework** due to inconsistent information between disciplines
- **Excessive change requests** due to information gaps
- **Models nobody uses** after the project is built

Projects with real BIM integration report:

- Significant reduction of field interferences
- Fewer RFIs and changes during construction
- Delivery of digital assets useful for operations and maintenance
- Greater certainty in cost and schedule estimates
